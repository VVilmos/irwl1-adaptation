\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

To evaluate the impact of gradual magnitude-based pruning on OOD and adversarial robustness, we conducted experiments using the
ResNet20 architecture and the CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-10-C datasets. The datasets were presented in section \ref{subsec:cifar10c}.

The architecture of ResNet20 is presented in Table \ref{tab:resnet20}. $C(n)$ denotes 2D convolutional layers with $n$ output channels, $BN$ denotes
batch normalization layers, $GAP$ represents a Global Average Pooling layer, while $FC(n)$ denotes a fully connected layer with $n$ output units. 
To mitigate the vanishing gradient problem, the architecture includes residual connections in each residual block, which are denoted by square brackets.

For gradual pruning, we applied a custom strategy which was inspired by the Iteratively Reweighted $l1$-minimization problem \cite{boyd}. By updating 
the weights of the regularization term after each epoch, the regularization was more effective in pushing weights towards zero then common 
$l1$-regularization. The training process consisted of the following phases: a "warm-up" phase of 20 epochs without the sparsifying regularization; 
the "regularization" phase to drive weights towards zero while fitting the data; a pruning step where weights with magnitudes below a certain threshold
were set to zero; finally a "re-fitting" phase were the remaining weights could be updated to refit the data without the constant pressure of 
the regularization term. As the process was gradual, these three phases were repeated until a sparsity level of $95\%$ was reached. For evaluation purposes, 
models were saved at every $5\%$ step in sparsity. Note that regularization term was defined on individual weights, not groups of weights.
The additional hyperparameters are listed in Table \ref{tab:training_params} in Appendix \ref{sec:appendix}.
