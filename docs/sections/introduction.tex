\section*{Introduction}

In numerous fields of industry - such as healthcare, computer vision, natural language processing, etc. - neural networks have been deployed
to facilitate various applications that were not possible before \cite{effnet}. However, the requirements for a deployable model are
manifold. Apart from being accurate enough to yield economic value, the model also needs to be energy efficient and adaptable
to hardware constraints, while being robust to maliciously altered inputs and changes in the input distribution. 

Our work focuses on a custom pruning strategy and examines its impact from a robustness perspective. By trying to examine robustness from different
angles, we aim to improve our understanding of the relationship between model sparsity and robustness, and support our future work on
improving our pruning strategy.  

After presenting the motivations and various definitions of robustness in Chapter \ref{sec:background}, we discuss the resources and algorithms we
used for our experiments in Chapter \ref{sec:method}. The results of our experiments are presented in Chapter \ref{sec:exp}, followed by
our summary and future research directions.
